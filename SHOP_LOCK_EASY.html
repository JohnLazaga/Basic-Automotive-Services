Locking BASIC to your shop devices — easy guide

A worker took the password her browser had saved and opened BASIC from a computer outside the shop. This guide closes that for good, by making BASIC ask the device to prove itself — something a password can never do.

What this does, in plain words

Today, anyone who has the password can open BASIC from anywhere. That is what a password is — it proves who you are, never where or on what.

After this guide, each shop iPad holds a secret key of its own, created inside the iPad's security chip. When a worker signs in, the server asks that iPad to sign a one-time puzzle. The right password with the wrong iPad gets nothing — not a slow page, not a warning: no data at all.

The part that matters: that key cannot be read, copied, screenshotted, emailed or written down — not by a worker, not by you. It is not stored as a file. It never leaves the iPad. This is the thing a saved password could never be.
This replaces Part A of the old Device Lockdown guide. That guide told you to type a secret password into each device and let it remember. Do not do that any more — that is exactly how the leak happened. The old guide's Guided Access section (locking an iPad to one app) is still good and still worth doing.

Before you start — check each iPad (5 minutes each)

Do these three checks on every iPad before enrolling anything. The third one is the one people get wrong.

1 Check the iPad is new enough

Settings → General → About → Software Version.

  • iPadOS 14 or higher — good, carry on.
  • iPadOS 13 or lower — this iPad cannot do the check. See If a device is too old at the end.
Your shop iPads are on 17.7.10. That is fine — no need to update them for this.

2 Check the passcode and Touch ID are on

Settings → Touch ID & Passcode (or Face ID & Passcode).

  • A passcode must be set — one only you and your admins know, not one the workers know.
  • iPhone/iPad Unlock must be switched on for Touch ID or Face ID.

3 Switch iCloud passwords OFF ← the important one

Best: the iPad has no Apple ID signed in at all. Check Settings — if the very top shows a name, an Apple ID is signed in.

If an Apple ID is signed in and you cannot remove it, then:

Settings → [the name at the top] → iCloud → Passwords and Keychain → OFF.

Why this matters so much. With iCloud passwords switched on, a key made on this iPad copies itself to every Apple device on that Apple ID — including someone's personal iPhone at home. That would hand the key to exactly the person we are locking out. With it off, the key is sealed into this one iPad's chip and can never leave it.
You cannot get this wrong silently. BASIC checks, and refuses to enrol a device whose key would sync. If you see the iCloud message during enrolment, come back and finish this step. It also keeps watching afterwards — if someone signs the iPad into iCloud next month, BASIC stops that device by itself.

Part A — one time, by John (the owner)

Do this once from the office computer, before anyone goes out to the branches. It changes nothing for staff — the lock stays off until you switch it on in Part C.

1 Send up the app

In the BASIC_by_JMSI foldernode deploy-branches.js --push

Builds all four branches, runs the tests, and pushes them live.

2 Send up the server side

Same folderC:\node20\node-v20.18.2-win-x64\firebase.cmd deploy --only functions,firestore:rules
If it complains about not being logged in, set HOME and USERPROFILE to C:\fbcli-home first — that is where your Firebase login is kept. Do not use tools\firebase.exe; it is broken.
Nothing has changed for staff yet. The lock is off until an administrator turns it on, and it cannot even be turned on until at least one device is enrolled. There is no way to accidentally lock out a branch from this step.

Part B — enrol each shop device

This is the on-site job. It must be done on the device itself, with it in your hands, by an administrator. About one minute per device.

1 Open BASIC on that device and sign in as an administrator

Your own admin account. Not a worker's.

2 Go to Settings → Shop devices

Tap ＋ Enrol this device.

3 Give it a name you will recognise later

“Fairview front desk iPad”, “Bay 2 tablet”, “Sudipen counter PC”. You will be reading this list a year from now when one of them breaks — a name like “iPad 3” will not help you.

4 Confirm with Touch ID / Face ID

The iPad asks you to confirm. That prompt is the iPad proving it is itself and making its key. On a Windows computer it asks for the Windows Hello PIN instead.

If you get “This device is signed into iCloud…”, stop. Go back and do the Check 3 step above, then enrol again. Do not skip it — enrolling that device would defeat the whole exercise.

5 Check it appears in the list, then do the next device

Repeat for every device your staff actually use to open BASIC. A device that is not on this list will not work once you finish Part C.

Do not enrol anything that leaves the building. A laptop that goes home with someone is an enrolled device sitting in their house. Enrol fixed counter iPads and shop computers only.

Part C — switch the lock on

Do this per branch, after every device at that branch is enrolled.

1 Settings → Shop devices → Turn enforcement ON

Read the confirmation and accept it. The banner at the top of the card turns green and says staff can only open BASIC on the devices listed.

2 Test it before you leave the branch

This is the step people skip and then regret. On a device that is not enrolled — your own phone is perfect — open the branch's BASIC address and sign in with a worker's account.

You should see “BASIC can only be opened on a shop device”. If you get in, enforcement is not on. Go back to step 1.

Then sign in on a shop iPad as a normal worker and confirm everyday work still behaves. Two minutes now saves a phone call at 7am.
You are not affected. Administrators and the owner are exempt on purpose, so you keep working from home. The lock applies to non-admin staff.

Part D — do you still need the shop-network lock?

There is a second, older lock in Settings → Shop network, which checks the shop's internet address instead of the device.

Our advice: leave it OFF and use devices only. Your internet provider changes the shop's address from time to time, and every change would lock the branch out until an admin re-taps a button — in Sudipen's case, from 250 km away. The device lock has no such problem: the iPad is the iPad wherever the internet address wanders.

Turn the network lock on as well only if you want a device that walks out of the building to stop working the moment it leaves. Both locks can run together; a worker then needs an enrolled device and the shop's Wi-Fi.

Everyday rules — keep it working

When this happensDo this
A new iPad or computer arrivesRun the three checks, then Part B on it. Until you do, staff cannot use it.
An iPad is lost or stolenSettings → Shop devices → tap ✕ next to it. It stops working within 20 minutes, even if it is already open in someone's hands.
An iPad is erased, reset or repairedIts key is gone. Remove the old entry (✕) and enrol it again as if it were new.

If a device is too old

An iPad on iPadOS 13 or older, or one with no Touch ID / Face ID at all, cannot do this check — no setting or update will fix it if the iPad cannot go past 13. Your options, best first:

  • Replace it. A basic current iPad is the cheapest and cleanest answer, and everything in this guide then applies to it.
  • Use a Windows computer at that station instead. Windows Hello with a PIN works the same way and is enrolled with the same steps.
  • Use the shop-network lock for that branch (Part D) instead of the device lock. Weaker, and you take on the changing-address problem — but it is better than nothing while you budget for hardware.
Do not leave the old iPad running with no lock as a workaround. One unlocked station is all it takes; the worker will simply use that one.

Quick checklist per device

  1. Settings → General → About → Software Version is 14 or higher
  2. Settings → Touch ID & Passcode → passcode set, Touch ID on for unlock
  3. Settings → [name] → iCloud → Passwords and Keychain OFF (or no Apple ID at all)
  4. Open BASIC on the device, sign in as an administrator
  5. Settings → Shop devices → ＋ Enrol this device → name it → confirm with Touch ID
  6. Confirm it now appears in the device list

Once per branch, after every device is done:

  1. Settings → Shop devices → Turn enforcement ON
  2. Test from a non-enrolled device — you must be refused
